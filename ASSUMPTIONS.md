# v0.01 implementation decisions

The addendum in `DESIGN.MD` supersedes earlier tentative lists and formulas. These choices resolve unspecified mechanics without adding classes, quests, mana, automapping, or durability. Balance/content tables are in `vale/data.py`.

## Character, time, and economy

- Start at Day 1, Morning (08:00), with no silver. The random weapon is equipped; three torches and a random Tier 1 item are carried. The item pool includes equipment and the eight specified utilities. Starting items are not dungeon treasure.
- HP is `5 × starting Might`. Increasing Might later improves damage, resistance, and future HP rewards; it does not retrospectively increase HP. Choosing the HP reward adds current Might to permanent maximum and current HP.
- Level thresholds continue with `50 × level × (level + 1)` XP to reach the next level. XP is cumulative; multiple earned rewards are chosen separately. Well Fed uses fractional XP and rounds maximum HP up so its small bonus matters. Damage multipliers round down after bonuses, before armor.
- First exploration of a room awards 2 XP. First reaching each new lifetime depth awards `5 × depth` XP. Stair/hole exits and rope shafts descend one depth; ordinary passages keep depth. Backtracking awards no XP.
- Travel takes 5 minutes; search takes 10; interacting, trading, and town services take 5; using items/equipment and combat rounds take 1. Rest takes 8 hours. Browsing menus takes no time. A dead character cannot recover during a rest that killed them.
- Inn and successful dungeon rest restore full HP but do not cure persistent afflictions. Dungeon rest has a 35% interruption chance, after 10–120 minutes; interrupted rest gives no healing.
- Prices are explicit prototype values in `PRICES`. Cunning improves both purchase and sale prices. The purchase multiplier is `max(1.05, 1.6 − .06 × Cunning)`; sale is `min(.95, .4 + .04 × Cunning)`. Positive-value items sell for at least 1 sp. These formulas are hidden in the game. There is no random price variation.
- Weekly stock supplies eight individual torches, one of every other utility, and six random equipment pieces. Stock refreshes at each seven-day boundary. Stock is finite, and sold items can be bought back. Buying back treasure never awards new extraction XP.
- Each drink risks Tipsy at `15 × today's drinks − 5 × Might` percent, clamped to 0–100. Tipsy lasts two hours and imposes −10 percentage points to hit. Rumors provide new information once per day; the game never labels rumor reliability.
- Treasure contributes its listed base value once to Fortune and XP, not its negotiated sale price. Monster parts, equipment and imported items do not count as Fortune. Loose Silver caches count once on safe extraction; monster-carried silver retains its existing spoils behavior. Monster-carried treasure does count. XP and Fortune are earned even if treasure is retained rather than sold.

## Equipment, combat, and effects

- Worn/Ragged weapon condition changes value, not weapon damage. Armor follows the final addendum: Worn is mechanically unchanged, Ragged reduces reduction by 1, Ruined gives no protection. Nothing degrades during play.
- One-handed weapons use Main Hand. Shield/lit Torch normally use Off Hand; with no weapon, Shield and Torch may occupy the two free hands. Two-handed weapons prohibit either. Equipping replaces an item in its slot without increasing inventory count; unequipping needs a free inventory slot. Equipment changes/dropping are unavailable during combat.
- Unequipped lit torches still burn but provide no usable light. Replacing a held torch with a fresh one discards the old held torch. Burned-out torches are removed. The HUD never shows exact remaining burn time.
- Unarmed attacks roll 1d2 plus the ordinary Might bonus. A torch fire attack consumes the action, uses normal accuracy/evasion without the spear bonus, deals 1d2 fire damage, and receives no Might bonus. Fire/acid bypass physical armor. Oil adds no initial damage; subsequent fire ignites an oiled target for 1d4 damage per enemy turn.
- Sword overflow is already post-armor damage and carries through surviving enemies in displayed order, without additional hit/armor rolls. A Zombie headshot requires all weapon dice to roll their natural maxima (12 on a 2d6 weapon), independent of modifiers.
- Cave Bat hit chance is 50%; Cave Bear's provisional tagline is “Its claws scrape the stone.” Its pelt is worth 8 sp. Ooze HP is rolled once on spawning and is always hidden.
- Goblins at half HP or lower spend their action attempting to flee, with 50% success. Successful flight removes that goblin from the encounter and grants no kill XP/loot.
- Spiders try their one Web on their first enemy action. A hit causes exactly one skipped player action; it deals no damage. Surviving enemies then act normally. Spider poison is checked only after penetrating physical damage.
- Spider Venom coats the actual equipped weapon and remains on it when unequipped. It lasts until that weapon's next successful hit, adds 1d2, and has a 50% chance of poisoning a monster. Monster poison deals 1 HP per enemy turn for that fight. The bottle is returned immediately upon application.
- Troll regeneration occurs before its action; any fire/acid damage suppresses regeneration for that expedition. Torchlight never petrifies. Generated daylight vaults remain lit for their expedition, even as the clock advances. Daylight petrification grants normal low kill XP but no loot.
- On successful flight, trolls have a 50% chance to follow through the incoming connection, allowing luring into an existing daylight room. Other enemies remain in their room with their damage preserved. Flight through the original outside entrance ends the expedition and extracts carried treasure. Otherwise successful flight returns through the connection used to enter the current room. Return to Vale is unavailable during active combat.
- Bless/Curse affect hit chance and damage for 24 hours; both can coexist and cancel. An unidentified magical item still applies its Bless/Curse effect while equipped. Random equipment can be magical (10% in the eligible equipment pool); there are no extra magical item types or cursed binding rules. Shrine curing clears poison, timed curses, and item curses; identification reliably reveals an inventory item's property. Equipped items can be unequipped for identification.
- Poison ticks hourly from the time it was applied, including the final hour, and lasts `max(0, 12 − Might)` hours. Reapplication refreshes duration without delaying an already scheduled tick. No extra disease system is introduced.
- Darkness adds −20 points to hit and −20 to search/trap detection; loose loot cannot be collected without light. Dark new rooms have a 25% chance of one extra monster and an independent 15% chance of two extras. Actual daylight suppresses these additions. Ordinary content tables remain unchanged.
- Scrolls contain one of three provisional single-use spells: Ember (1d4 + Magic fire), Acid (same acid damage), or Mending (1d4 + Magic healing). Anyone can activate them with no generic roll. Characters have no learned spells in v0.01, and Cast reports that; there is no mana or spell-learning system.

## Dungeon and unresolved content

- Room templates are uniformly selected. Every room generates 1d4 ordinary exits, including its incoming connection if there is one. Consequently dead ends exist. Secret doors, unlocked doors, and usable shafts can add exits. The back connection is always visible; there is no grid or map.
- Treasure/threat tables retain the addendum probabilities. Ordinary content uses the v0.01a patch weights 30/27/17/10/5/5/6; secret content is unchanged. Ordinary Treasure rolls have a 15% chance of a 1d6+2 loose Silver cache; the other 85% use the unchanged treasure table. Secret treasure weights, in the listed treasure order, are `10/10/15/15/15/15/12/8`. Hidden features have a 10% chance and are equally split between a Secret Door and a cache. Searching is `12 × Cunning` percent before darkness, and can be repeated at its normal time/light cost.
- Features and special interactions are optional and resolve once per generated element. A chest/door can be retried if its lock did not open. Locks choose difficulty 10/20/30. Failed attempts at least ten points beyond the success threshold break the Pick.
- Corpse outcomes are 50% nothing, 15% item, 10% loose Silver cache, 15% Zombie, 10% trap. Other specified feature tables retain their tentative percentages. A successful chest can still be empty. A shaft variant in the Damp Chamber has a 35% chance to offer a rope descent; all template shafts can be inspected, but the remaining 65% lead only to drains.
- Basic carried drops use 50/25/20/5 for nothing/silver/item/treasure and 1d6 silver. Improved Ogre/Troll drops use 30/25/20/25. Rat/Bat/Bear materials and Spider Venom require harvesting. Goblin/Skeleton equipment is shown in the resulting loot; no separate durability stats are exposed.
- Undetected traps trigger on first arrival. Detected traps can be avoided by leaving or actively disarmed using the detection formula. Rope guarantees making a detected Pitfall safe. Pitfalls do not reposition the player in this prototype. Physical armor applies to all three traps; arrows check venom only on penetrating damage.
- The fountain's reserved result is harmless strange flavor. It is not a light resource despite its description. Fresco lore uses a short atmospheric passage; a reveal outcome uncovers a pre-existing hidden feature, if any.
- Picking up the Human Baby takes an inventory slot and produces ambiguous text. No hidden quest/consequence is invented. The Tavern Sign has only flavor. Wounded animals can be helped or put out of their misery for material; helping has 30% risk of hostility and otherwise the creature leaves. There is no companion system.
- The Pale Book can be read once in place or taken for later single-use reading. Reading uses its specified table; its scroll-like outcome immediately resolves one provisional spell, and its XP outcome gives 10 XP. No monster target means an offensive spell dissolves harmlessly.

## Persistence and limitations

- Saves are versioned atomic JSON, including the expedition and random-generator state. The Graveyard is stored separately and includes name, Fortune, deepest depth, in-game day, and cause. Death is recorded before the active save is invalidated; repeated recording is idempotent. Restoring a save for a character already recorded dead is rejected.
- This is local single-player persistence, not tamper-proof scoring. One active character is stored per data directory. New Game can explicitly replace it without adding a false death record. Future save migrations are not implemented.
- Windows uses the standard Python launcher; no platform-specific dependencies are present. The automated suite and terminal smoke test were executed on Linux/Python 3.12. Native Windows execution still needs playtester confirmation.
- Durability, self-identification, additional blessings, learned spells, new threat tiers, stricter return navigation, and expanded baby/book consequences remain future work as allowed by the design. All specified v0.01 systems and content are present; unresolved outcomes use the simple defaults above.


## v0.01a terminal and template interactions

- Prose wraps at word boundaries at a configurable default of 76 columns, capped to terminal width. Interactive terminals redraw; redirected transcripts remain readable. The last action stays visible while browsing until another action emits results.
- Vale destinations loop until Leave; Inventory returns to its caller. The saved town_screen field resumes a town location after interruption. New optional fields default when reading old version-1 saves.
- Template objects are separate saved elements, added even on Empty rolls. Each resolves once; unrelated generated features retain their behavior. Statue and casket, courtyard plants and centerpiece, and temple altar/glyphs/statuette can be examined separately.
- Sarcophagi resolve 45% nothing, 20% treasure, 20% undead, 10% undead plus treasure, 5% strange atmosphere. Other searchable objects mostly yield nothing; pools are in CONTEXT_OUTCOMES. Ruined equipment and stone/architectural objects yield atmosphere only. Underwater interactions can be empty, dangerous, or reveal existing content/navigation.
- Cash caches take no inventory slot. Collecting adds spendable silver and an expedition extraction tally; safe return or successful entrance flight grants its XP/Fortune once and clears the tally. Carried-drop cash is unchanged and already available on appropriate humanoids. Corpses and rotten chests can now yield cache coins.
